The Elegance of Multi-Head Thread Embroidery

30 August 2026 · 4 min read

Modern formal wear lives or dies on detail, and nothing states luxury more plainly than multi-head thread embroidery.

The technique is both traditional and industrial. A multi-head machine runs several embroidery heads in parallel, each carrying its own thread colour, all stitching the same design at the same time with the same coordinates. The result is a dense, layered, slightly raised surface — the kind you can feel with a fingertip — instead of the flat printed effect that cheaper production settles for.

That matters most on the fabrics we work with. Lawn, cotton, viscose, dhank and khaddar all take thread differently. A lightweight summer lawn needs a lighter stitch density or it puckers around the motif; a heavy viscose can carry far more weight without distorting, which is why our winter necklines are more elaborate than our summer ones.

Where the work goes

On a typical RANG DATA shirt you will find three zones of embroidery:

  • The neckline and front panel — the heaviest concentration, since it sits at eye level.
  • Sleeve borders, or pattis — narrow repeating bands that frame the arm without adding bulk.
  • Daman and hem detailing — lighter work that keeps the eye moving down the garment.

Placement is decided before the dye is finalised, not after. When embroidery is designed against a colour rather than dropped onto it, thread and ground reinforce each other; when it is added afterwards, the motif tends to float.

Thread quality is the quiet variable

Two suits can carry identical designs and age completely differently. Cheap rayon thread loses its sheen after two or three washes and can bleed into the base cloth. We use premium threads that hold their shine, which is a large part of why an embroidered suit costs what it does.

Good threadwork should still look deliberate after a season of wear, not just on the day it arrives.

If you are shopping for a family gathering or a formal event, look for stitch density around the neckline, whether motifs are aligned with the fabric grain, and how the reverse side of the panel is finished. Those three tells will tell you more than any product description.

Every design in our current edits is embroidered this way. Browse the winter collection for the heavier work, or the summer collection for lighter daytime pieces.
